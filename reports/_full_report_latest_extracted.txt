Development and Evaluation of a Retrieval-Augmented Generation System in the Turkish Legal Domain

General Summary
In this project, a Retrieval-Augmented Generation system operating in the Turkish legal domain was developed and the different stages of the system were compared on the same benchmark. The main focus of the study is not only to build a model that produces answers, but to create a legal RAG architecture that can find the correct legal document and article, ground its answer in a source, generate citations, and measure hallucination risk.
The project started with an old Kaggle-only baseline and was later improved through data cleaning, corpus normalization, dense retrieval, sparse retrieval, hybrid retrieval, reranker, embedding tuning, reranker tuning, and LLM fine-tuning stages. In the final system selection, not only answer-overlap metrics such as token F1 or ROUGE-L were considered, but also citation accuracy, grounded citation score, and hallucination risk.

Dataset and Data Source
The main legal data used in the project was muhammetakkurt/mevzuat-gov-dataset. This dataset consists of legal texts extracted from mevzuat.gov.tr, the official legislation portal of the Turkish state. The dataset includes laws, regulations, and amendment texts; each record contains not only text content, but also metadata information belonging to the legal document. The dataset page states that the data was extracted from the mevzuat.gov.tr source and that the legal texts are provided together with detailed article structures. (huggingface.co)
According to the dataset information on Hugging Face, the data is in JSON format, is presented as a text modality, is shared with the legal tag, and appears under the MIT license. The dataset page shows that the default split consists of 907 rows and that the total file size is approximately 37.3 MB. (huggingface.co)

Data Leakage and Benchmark Separation
The 190-question benchmark used in the project was kept separate from the tuning and development processes. The data used in retrieval tuning, embedding tuning, reranker tuning, and LLM fine-tuning stages was separated from the final benchmark evaluation.
This separation is important because using the same questions in both tuning and test stages may cause system performance to appear higher than it actually is. Therefore, in the final evaluation, all systems were compared on the same 190-question test benchmark. This allowed a more reliable performance comparison between the baseline, tuned, and fully optimized systems.
1. Project Objective
The objective of this project is to gradually improve a Retrieval-Augmented Generation system operating in the Turkish legal domain and measure both retrieval and answer generation quality. The system was designed not only as a structure that produces answers, but also as a legal RAG architecture that can find the correct legal source, rely on the relevant article, generate citations, and measure hallucination risk.
Therefore, throughout the project, different corpus structures, embedding models, retrieval methods, reranker approaches, and LLM fine-tuning experiments were compared on the same benchmark. Thus, which component truly contributed to the system, which stage did not produce the expected improvement, and which system was more reliable in the end were evaluated quantitatively.
2. Benchmark and Data Structure
A 190-question gold benchmark was used in the project. This benchmark consists of Turkish legal questions, and for each question, a verified answer, the relevant legal document, and article mapping were stored.
Thanks to this structure, the system could be evaluated not only in terms of answer quality, but also in terms of its success in finding the correct document and the correct article. In the legal domain, answer generation alone is not sufficient; the legal source on which the answer is based must also be correct. Therefore, the benchmark made it possible to measure the retrieval and generation stages jointly.

7.1 Fine-Tuning Data Sources
The fine-tuning experiments in this project used the external training files provided under the data/external directory. These files were not used as the final official retrieval corpus. Instead, they were used only for adaptation experiments in order to test whether embedding tuning, reranker tuning, and LLM fine-tuning could improve the RAG system.
The first fine-tuning setup used the original external files directly. The main files were data/external/embedding.jsonl for embedding adaptation, data/external/reranker.jsonl for reranker adaptation, and data/external/llm.jsonl for LLM instruction tuning. In addition, data/external/gold_benchmark.json was inspected as an external benchmark-style resource.
These experiments showed that fine-tuning could improve some generation-side metrics, especially Token F1 and ROUGE-L. However, retrieval-side tuning did not outperform the zero-shot Qwen3 retrieval stack.
A second, stricter tuning setup was later prepared. In this setup, external examples were mapped to the official-law article schema only when their law and article identifiers could be matched to the normalized official corpus. This produced a cleaner and more methodologically defensible tuning set, because the final locked benchmark was not used for training and the examples were aligned with the official article_key and citation structure.

9.2 Interpretation of Token F1 and ROUGE-L
The Token F1 and ROUGE-L scores remain relatively low in absolute terms because the gold answers and generated RAG answers have different styles. The gold answers are short reference answers, while the generated outputs are intentionally longer, structured, and citation-aware legal explanations.
For example, the RAG answer often includes a short answer, a detailed explanation, supporting legal articles, and limitations. These additional sections may be legally useful, but they are penalized by lexical-overlap metrics because they introduce tokens that are not present in the short gold answer.
Therefore, Token F1 and ROUGE-L were not interpreted alone. They were evaluated together with semantic similarity, citation accuracy, grounded citation score, and hallucination-proxy metrics. From a user-safety perspective, this distinction is important. A longer and well-structured legal answer may increase the user’s perceived trust in the system because it appears more complete and authoritative. However, in legal QA, this can also create a risk of false confidence if the answer is not correctly grounded in legal sources. Therefore, answer similarity metrics were interpreted together with citation and grounding metrics to ensure that fluent or convincing answers were not treated as reliable unless they were also source-supported.

14. Custom Data Pipeline
The project also supports instructor-provided custom document collections and benchmark files in both local and Google Drive/Colab-based workflows. For this purpose, a single-entry evaluation script, run_teacher_eval.py, and a teacher-facing notebook, notebooks/25_teacher_custom_data_single_notebook_demo.ipynb, were prepared. The instructor only needs to place custom documents under data/custom_docs/ and the benchmark file under data/custom_benchmark/custom_benchmark.csv. The system then automatically ingests the custom documents, converts them into the project retrieval schema, builds a custom index, and evaluates the same RAG pipeline on the provided benchmark. The same workflow can also be executed in Google Drive and Colab by uploading the repository folder to Drive and running either run_teacher_eval.py or notebooks/25_teacher_custom_data_single_notebook_demo.ipynb.
By default, this evaluation path uses the strongest final system configuration of the project: Qwen/Qwen3-Embedding-8B for dense retrieval, Qwen/Qwen3-Reranker-8B for reranking, and Qwen/Qwen3-32B for answer generation. If gold document/article fields are present, the system reports retrieval metrics such as hit/recall, MRR, and nDCG. If gold answers are also present, it additionally reports answer-level, citation-grounding, and hallucination-proxy metrics. The main results are written to output files, while the key metrics are also printed directly to the console so that the instructor can inspect them without manually opening intermediate CSV or JSON artifacts.

18. Limitations and Future Work
Although strong results were obtained in this study, there are some limitations. First, the benchmark is limited to 190 questions. Although this number provides a sufficient starting point for comparing systems, a broader benchmark covering different legal fields would increase the generalizability of the results.
Second, citation and hallucination analyses are largely based on deterministic proxy metrics and LLM judge evaluation. Although this approach provides systematic measurement, supporting it with expert human evaluation in the legal domain would further increase final reliability.
Third, fine-tuning experiments did not always provide the expected improvement. In particular, embedding tuning and reranker tuning could not surpass the zero-shot Qwen3 retrieval stack. This shows that the quality of tuning data, its alignment with the task, and the selection of hard negatives should be optimized more carefully.
The following improvements can be made in future work:
Development Area
Description
Larger benchmark
More questions from different legal fields can be added.
Human expert evaluation
Answers and citations can be checked by lawyers or legal experts.
Better tuning data
Higher-quality hard negative sets can be prepared for embedding and reranker tuning.
Citation verification
The article-level correctness of generated citations can be measured separately and manually.
Real usage testing
The system can be tested with sample questions in law office scenarios.
Field-based analysis
Separate performance reports can be produced for fields such as labor law, commercial law, and tenancy law.

These limitations do not indicate that the system is unsuccessful; rather, they show the areas where the current study can be further strengthened. The current results show that the system forms a strong foundation, especially in terms of retrieval and grounded generation.
Fine-Tuning Did Not Always Improve Performance
The experiments showed that fine-tuning does not automatically improve every component of a RAG system. While QLoRA fine-tuning improved answer-overlap metrics and slightly improved semantic similarity, embedding and reranker tuning did not outperform the zero-shot Qwen3 retrieval stack.
This result is important because the Qwen3 embedding and reranker models already provide strong zero-shot retrieval performance. When the tuning data is smaller, noisier, or not perfectly aligned with the target benchmark distribution, fine-tuning may narrow the model's behavior and reduce general retrieval quality.
Therefore, the final system was selected according to measured benchmark performance rather than the assumption that the most fine-tuned system must be the best system.

19. General Conclusion
This project has shown that developing a reliable RAG system in the Turkish legal domain cannot be solved only by increasing model size or performing fine-tuning. The largest gain was obtained by correctly structuring the data and carefully optimizing the retrieval architecture.
At the beginning, although the Kaggle-only system appeared partially reasonable on the document-level side, it was quite weak in terms of article-level grounding. With corpus normalization, the official-law article corpus v3 structure, strong dense retrieval, and Qwen3-Reranker-8B, article-level retrieval was significantly improved. While article_hit@5 was 0.0526 in the Kaggle-only baseline, this value reached 0.837 in the final retrieval stack.
On the generation side, the effect of fine-tuning was more complex. Gemma LoRA did not improve overall quality; Qwen3-32B QLoRA increased token_f1 and rouge_l metrics, but produced a riskier result than the base model in terms of citation accuracy and grounding. Therefore, the final system was selected not as the QLoRA system with the highest lexical overlap score, but as Qwen3-32B Base RAG + best retrieval stack, which is safer in terms of citation-grounding and low hallucination risk.
As a result, the study presents a measurable, comparable, and reliability-oriented legal RAG system in the Turkish legal domain. In the project, all stages from corpus normalization to retrieval ablations, from fine-tuning experiments to hallucination analysis were evaluated on the same benchmark, and the final system was determined according to these measurements.

22. Qualitative Comparison of Base and Fine-Tuned Responses 

In our qualitative comparison on the question of individual application to the Constitutional Court, the base Qwen3-32B system produced a more structured and practically useful answer than the fine-tuned variant. While the fine-tuned model tended to provide a broader and more verbose explanation, it also introduced a noisier list of supporting provisions and less focused legal grounding. By contrast, the base model gave a clearer procedural summary, organized the conditions more coherently, and relied on a more disciplined citation pattern. This example is consistent with our quantitative findings: although QLoRA fine-tuning improved overlap-based metrics such as Token F1 and ROUGE-L on average, the base model remained stronger in citation-grounding quality and overall legal reliability.
